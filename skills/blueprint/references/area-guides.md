# Area guides and AI search

## Area guides

`python scripts/blueprint.py guide <district>` gives the facts: the market, rents, property types and new builds,
conservation areas, national park or National Landscape, stations, schools nearby, broadband and flood shares.

Agents who serve several towns struggle to be found in all of them. A good area guide for each town, on the agent's
own website, is what search engines and AI assistants pick up.

Structure for a website area page (aim for 600 to 900 words):

1. **A plain opening:** where it is, what it's like, who lives there. The agent's local knowledge, in their voice.
2. **Homes:** the mix of property types, typical prices by type (medians with counts, registered sales, period), the
   price trend for the council area, new-build schemes by street.
3. **Renting:** the council's average rents by bedrooms (for areas with lettings).
4. **Getting around:** stations by name; the agent adds journey times they know.
5. **Schools:** that there are primary and secondary schools nearby, with names only if the agent wants them and no
   claims about places or ratings.
6. **Character:** conservation areas, parks and green belt by name.
7. **The agent:** one short paragraph and how to get in touch.
8. **Sources and date** at the foot.

Rules: no crime claims, no flood "warnings" about named streets, no promises about schools, no house numbers, every
figure with its source and month. Write as a local guide first and an agent second.

## The AI-visibility check

Buyers and sellers increasingly ask AI assistants things like "best estate agents in Sale" or "who should I sell my
house with in Altrincham". If the user asks how they show up:

1. If you have web search, run five to eight questions a local buyer or seller would ask (the town, the agent's
   specialisms, "best", "recommended", "who sold the most", lettings) and note which agents are named and which
   sources are cited.
2. Report plainly: where the user appears, who appears instead, and the sources behind the answers (their own site,
   portals, review sites, local press, forums).
3. Suggest what the user can do themselves: area guides for each town they serve, an up-to-date Google Business
   Profile, reviews, consistent name and address everywhere, helpful answers in local forums under their own name,
   and published market updates with sources.
4. Be honest about limits: results vary by assistant, by day and by person, and nobody can guarantee a mention.

Without web search, explain the method and give them the questions to try themselves.
